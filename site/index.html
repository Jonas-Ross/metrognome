        A brain and a pair of ears

        selecta

        The brain. TypeScript, runs on your Mac.

        • Caches your whole Apple Music library: tracks, playlists, play and skip history.
        • Gives Claude tools to search it, inspect a tracklist and draft a playlist.
        • Writes the finished playlist back to Music, and only when you say save.

        metrognome

        The ears. Rust, the engine you just used.

        • Finds tempo and key from a track's 30-second preview clip, since the music in your library is DRM-protected.
        • Every number ships with a confidence, a source and a flag when it is a guess.
        • Also a standalone CLI that prints JSON, if all you want is BPM and key.

        House rules

        Wrong data is worse than missing

        An estimate carries its confidence and where it came from. A shaky one is flagged as a hint, and a flagged key is never stored as fact.

        Audio is heard, then forgotten

        Clips are analyzed in memory and dropped. No audio is written to disk, here or on your Mac.

        No taste in the tools

        Selecta never ranks or recommends. It reports facts and does what it is told. Taste belongs to you and your model.

        Local-first

        Your library lives in a database on your Mac. Only artist and title go out, to look up previews and public metadata.

        Get it

        Selecta is early and not packaged yet. A Homebrew install is on the way. Until then it builds from source on a Mac with Node and Rust.
